# Educational Progress Framework

Purpose: Define meaningful progress in AzubiForge.

Progress is not only completed chapters.

True progress means growing AP1 readiness.

---

## Why Chapter Completion Is Not Enough

A student can click "completed" after reading a chapter.

But reading is not the same as learning.

AzubiForge progress should consider:

- Understanding.
- Vocabulary.
- Exercises.
- Revision.
- Self-confidence.
- AP1 readiness.

---

## Progress Dimensions

### 1. Content Progress

Question:

How many chapters has the student studied?

Evidence:

- Chapter read.
- Summary reviewed.
- Checklist completed.

This is the simplest progress dimension.

---

### 2. Understanding Progress

Question:

Can the student explain the topic?

Evidence:

- Self-explanation.
- Correct intermediate exercises.
- Ability to give examples.

Understanding is stronger than reading.

---

### 3. Vocabulary Progress

Question:

Can the student recognize and use German AP1 terms?

Evidence:

- Vocabulary table reviewed.
- Terms used in answers.
- German task wording understood.

Vocabulary progress is essential because AP1 is in German.

---

### 4. Exercise Progress

Question:

Can the student solve questions?

Evidence:

- Easy exercises solved.
- Intermediate exercises solved.
- AP1-style tasks solved.
- Answers explained.

Exercise progress shows whether knowledge can be used.

---

### 5. Revision Progress

Question:

Has the student repeated the topic after time?

Evidence:

- Same-day review.
- Next-day review.
- Weekly review.
- Mistake correction.

Revision prevents forgetting.

---

### 6. Confidence Progress

Question:

Does the student feel able to face AP1 tasks?

Evidence:

- Student can explain without panic.
- Student knows what to do when a question is difficult.
- Student can identify weak topics.

Confidence must be based on evidence, not hope.

---

### 7. AP1 Readiness

Question:

Can the student solve AP1-style scenarios?

Evidence:

- Correct scenario answers.
- Correct use of task verbs.
- Clear justifications.
- Ability to compare and decide.

AP1 readiness is the final goal.

---

## Progress Levels

### Level 0: Not Started

Student has not studied the topic.

### Level 1: Seen

Student has read the topic but cannot explain it yet.

### Level 2: Understood

Student can explain the topic simply.

### Level 3: Practiced

Student solved exercises and reviewed answers.

### Level 4: Reviewed

Student repeated the topic after time.

### Level 5: AP1 Ready

Student can solve scenario-based AP1 questions.

---

## Rules For Progress

### Rule 1

Completed chapter does not automatically mean AP1 ready.

### Rule 2

Vocabulary must be learned actively.

### Rule 3

Exercises are mandatory for real progress.

### Rule 4

Revision is part of progress, not optional.

### Rule 5

Confidence must come from successful retrieval and practice.

---

## Recommended Progress Evaluation

For every chapter, evaluate:

- Did I read it?
- Did I understand it?
- Did I learn vocabulary?
- Did I solve exercises?
- Did I review mistakes?
- Can I solve AP1-style tasks?

Only when all are YES should the topic be considered strong.

---

## Course-Level Progress

A module is strong when:

- All chapters are understood.
- Key vocabulary is known.
- Common mistakes are clear.
- Mixed exercises can be solved.

The full AP1 course is strong when:

- The student can solve mixed tasks across modules.
- The student can explain decisions.
- The student can read German tasks confidently.
